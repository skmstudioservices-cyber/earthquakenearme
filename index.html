Did you just feel an earthquake?

  • Drop, Cover, Hold On: get under a sturdy table, protect your head and neck.
  • Stay away from windows, mirrors and heavy objects that can fall.
  • If you're outside: move to open ground, away from buildings, trees and power lines.
  • Aftershocks can follow the main quake — expect them and stay alert.
  • Use text/WhatsApp instead of calls — networks congest after strong quakes.
  • Emergency numbers (India): 112 (all-in-one), 108 (ambulance).

Why India gets earthquakes

Roughly 59% of India's land is prone to earthquakes: the entire Himalayan belt (from Kashmir to Arunachal Pradesh) sits where the Indian plate pushes into the Eurasian plate, the Andaman–Nicobar region lies near an active subduction zone, and the Kutch area of Gujarat has a history of major quakes (Bhuj 2001). Most South Asian quakes in this list cluster along the Hind Kush–Pamir region (Afghanistan–Tajikistan), one of the world's most active inland seismic zones.
